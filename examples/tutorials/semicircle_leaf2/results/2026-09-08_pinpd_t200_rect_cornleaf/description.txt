First rectangular (corn-leaf) tissue model. Replaces semicircular geometry with a
12 x 40 rectangular grid of square cells (dx=dy=1.0). The tissue is centered at x=0,
with y=0 at the base (sink) and y=40 at the tip (sources), approximating a corn leaf
blade oriented base-to-tip.

Key changes from previous run (2026-08-23 staggered semicircle):
- Geometry: semicircle (10 rings x 50 cols) replaced by rectangle (40 rows x 12 cols)
- SOURCE_WIDTH: 5 cells wide -> 1 cell wide (single-cell source zones, more corn-like)
- Source positions: cols 1, 3, 6, 8, 10 across the 12-col tip row
- k_growth_x: 0.002 -> 0.001 (narrower lateral growth)
- k_growth_y: 0.006 -> 0.004 (moderate apical elongation)
- Staggered zone activation unchanged: Z1 center (t=0), Z2 flanking (t~50), Z3 outer (t~100)

Biological rationale: corn (maize) leaves are monocot blades with parallel venation.
Veins run from the leaf tip (source zones) toward the base (vascular bundle / sink).
The rectangular geometry produces straighter, more parallel potential vein paths than
the semicircle. The narrow source zones (1 cell wide) reflect the thin vein initiation
zones described in the 2021 paper for growing leaves.

Result: tissue grew from 480 to 960 cells by t=200. Check wall variable 2 (Dij) in
ParaView for parallel strand formation from tip sources toward base sink.
